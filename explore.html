The Story Behind The Bowl

What Is Wakye?

A humble bowl of rice and beans that grew into one of Ghana's most beloved national dishes — here's the story, the culture, and the craft behind it.

What Is Wakye?

Rice and beans, transformed.

Wakye (pronounced "wah-chay") is a Ghanaian dish of rice and beans, usually black-eyed peas or cow beans, cooked together until tender. Traditionally eaten for breakfast or lunch, it's one of the most recognisable street foods in the country.

What sets it apart is its distinctive reddish-brown colour — achieved not with food colouring, but with dried sorghum leaf sheaves (locally called mbrehyie or sorghum stalks) and a pinch of kaun (edible potash), simmered with the rice and beans before being removed.

The name itself comes from the Dagbani language of northern Ghana, referring to a particular type of bean — a small clue to where this dish truly began.

History & Origins

From the north, to the nation.

Wakye is believed to have originated among the Mole-Dagbon people of northern Ghana, where rice and beans were dietary staples long before the dish became a household name in Accra and Kumasi.

As Ghanaians migrated south for work and trade, wakye travelled with them — carried into Zongo communities in the country's major cities, where it was sold by roadside vendors and quickly adopted well beyond its northern roots.

Today, it's eaten by people of every region and background, cutting across Ghana's ethnic and religious lines in a way few dishes do — a genuine national unifier, one bowl at a time.

Traditional Preparation

Slow-cooked, the honest way.

Rice and beans are simmered together in one pot with dried sorghum leaves and a touch of kaun, which gently release their colour and flavour into the grains as they cook.

Once the beans are tender and the rice has taken on its signature reddish tint, the sorghum leaves are removed — leaving behind a naturally coloured, subtly earthy base ready to be dressed with your choice of protein and sides.

At Wakye Point, we prepare fresh batches daily, keeping the process as close to tradition as possible while adding the generous protein options our customers love.

Culture Behind Wakye

More than a meal.

Wakye is deeply woven into everyday Ghanaian life — a breakfast for schoolchildren, a lunch for office workers, and a late-night favourite for anyone craving something filling and familiar.

It's traditionally sold wrapped in banana leaves by roadside vendors, often paired with an ever-expanding list of accompaniments: fried plantain, spaghetti ("talia"), shito, boiled eggs, avocado, wele, and a rich stew or protein of your choice.

That spirit of customisation is exactly what inspired the Wakye Point menu — the same beloved base, dressed the way you want it.
